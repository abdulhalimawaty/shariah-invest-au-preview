How the screen works

Two independent tests, both drawn from AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 21 — the framework most Islamic finance institutions and screening platforms build on. A stock has to clear both to be marked compliant.

1. Business activity

The company's core business can't be conventional banking or insurance, alcohol, gambling, pork, adult content, weapons, or tobacco. This is a qualitative, sector-level check applied before any ratio math.

2. Financial ratios

  • Interest-bearing debt < 30% of market cap
  • Cash & interest-bearing securities < 30% of market cap
  • Accounts receivable < 30% of market cap — the ratio unique to AAOIFI, missing from most other screens
  • Impermissible income (interest, gambling, etc.) < 5% of revenue
What v1 still doesn't cover: AAOIFI's fourth test measures impermissible income (interest, gambling, etc.) directly from segment revenue disclosures, which most companies don't break out cleanly — that test is currently approximated via the business-activity screen and spot checks rather than computed for every stock. Two patterns worth knowing: (1) companies like Computershare that explicitly disclose interest-type income as its own line — there, we used the real figure (23.4% of revenue, confirmed from FY26 results); (2) pre-revenue exploration companies (marked "review" among the miners above), where reported "revenue" is often just interest earned on capital-raise cash sitting in term deposits — a clean debt/cash/receivables screen completely misses this, since the balance sheet looks fine while the income composition doesn't. Debt, cash and receivables — the three ratios AAOIFI actually computes from the balance sheet — are checked in full for every stock marked pass, fail or review. Receivables was only skipped for names that already fail on debt or cash, since one breach is enough to disqualify regardless. Treat "compliant" here as a strong, methodical first pass, not a fatwa. Coverage: the stocks above are hand-screened with live figures pulled September 2026, including names like Rio Tinto, Amcor, ResMed, Newmont and News Corp whose ASX line is a secondary listing — for those, figures are converted from their primary overseas exchange rather than skipped. The 15 names still queued below are almost all NZX-primary companies (Fisher & Paykel Healthcare, Spark, Ryman, etc.) whose data isn't available through this build's data source yet.
