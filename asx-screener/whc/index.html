What these numbers mean

The binding constraint is interest-bearing debt at 33.3% of market cap — 3.3 percentage points above the 30% ceiling. Bringing that back inside the limit would take roughly a 10% reduction in that figure, or an equivalent rise in market capitalisation, with everything else held constant. The other test passes: cash and interest-bearing securities at 11.2%.

Across the 11 Energy companies screened on this site, 2 currently pass all three ratio tests.

How this verdict was reached

Whitehaven Coal was checked in two stages. First a business-activity screen: a company whose core business is conventional finance, alcohol, gambling, pork, adult entertainment, weapons or tobacco is excluded outright, whatever its balance sheet looks like. Whitehaven Coal clears that stage, so the three financial ratios above decide the outcome — each measured against market capitalisation and each capped at 30% under AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 21.

The receivables test is the one that most often separates this screen from others: Dow Jones and MSCI don't apply it at all, so a stock can be compliant on those standards and fail here. Figures come from public filings and are re-checked each quarter — they age as companies report.
