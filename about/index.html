Why this site exists

How this was researched and written

Most halal investing tools hand you a badge and ask you to trust it. This one shows the ratios, the sources and the judgment calls — including the ones we're not certain about.

The problem this was built for

If you're an Australian Muslim trying to work out whether a specific ASX stock is Shariah-compliant, whether your super is invested in things you'd rather it wasn't, or how much zakat you actually owe on shares, the existing options are thin. Global screening apps cover Australian stocks shallowly and rarely show their working. Local information is scattered across forum posts and marketing pages. Almost nothing explains why a verdict is what it is.

This site is an attempt to fix that for one market — Australia — and to be transparent enough that you can disagree with it on the evidence rather than on faith.

How the figures are produced

Every stock is screened against AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 21, the strictest of the three mainstream frameworks. Two stages: a business-activity screen that excludes conventional finance, alcohol, gambling, pork, adult entertainment, weapons and tobacco outright; then three balance-sheet ratios — debt, cash and interest-bearing securities, and accounts receivable — each capped at 30% of market capitalisation.

Figures come from public company filings and are re-checked quarterly. The full criteria, the exact thresholds, and links to the primary source documents are on the Methodology page. Nothing here is a proprietary score you have to take on trust — if you disagree with a threshold, you can go and read the same document we did.

The rules this site holds itself to

Show the working

Every verdict comes with the numbers behind it and the specific test that decided it. A badge with no reasoning is not an answer.

Say when it's disputed

Where credentialed scholars genuinely disagree — home finance structures, nisab standards, zakat on modern assets — both positions are presented rather than one being quietly picked for you.

Don't publish what can't be verified

A widely-repeated claim about an ASX-listed Islamic ETF was checked, couldn't be substantiated, and was left out — with the reasoning published instead.

Correct things in public

When something here turns out to be wrong, it gets fixed and the correction is stated plainly rather than edited away silently.

Name the gaps

Where the Australian market genuinely doesn't offer a good option — takaful, retail sukuk access — that's reported as a finding, not skipped because it's inconvenient.

No black boxes

The methodology is published in full. Anyone should be able to reproduce a verdict from the same filings.

What this site is not

It is not financial advice, and it is not a fatwa. Nobody here is licensed to advise you on your circumstances, and screening a stock against a standard is not the same as a scholar ruling on your situation. For anything consequential — a mortgage, an SMSF, an estate plan, a significant zakat calculation — the right move is a licensed adviser and a qualified scholar, ideally both.

It also doesn't sell anything. There are no accounts, no paywalled content, no sponsored placements and no paid rankings. Funds and providers listed on this site are there because they exist and are relevant, not because they paid to be. If that ever changes — an affiliate arrangement, for instance — it will be disclosed on the page where it applies, not buried here.

Common questions

Why AAOIFI rather than Dow Jones or MSCI?

Because it's the strictest of the three on the test that matters most in practice. AAOIFI checks accounts receivable against market cap; Dow Jones Islamic and MSCI Islamic don't check receivables at all. A stock can be compliant on those standards and fail here — that comparison is set out in full in a guide.

How current are the figures?

They're re-checked quarterly against public filings, and every screened stock page carries the ratios it was assessed on. Company balance sheets move, so a verdict is a point-in-time reading, not a permanent label.

Does the site store anything I type into the calculators?

No. The zakat, growth and tax calculators run entirely in your browser — nothing you enter is transmitted, stored or visible to anyone. The privacy policy lists every third party that sees a request when you use the site, including the two price services the zakat calculator calls.

I think a verdict here is wrong. What do I do?

Tell us, with the filing reference. Screening involves judgment calls — particularly for diversified groups where an excluded activity is a small share of revenue — and a well-argued correction is genuinely welcome. The methodology page shows exactly where each line is drawn, which is usually the fastest way to identify where a disagreement actually sits.

Is any of this a substitute for asking a scholar?

No, and it isn't trying to be. The aim is to get you to a scholar with the right question and the actual numbers in hand, instead of a vague worry.
