Halal ETFs on the ASX: what's actually listed

Five ETFs, one issuer, and a widely-repeated claim about a sixth that we checked and couldn't stand behind.

If you'd rather hold a fund than pick individual stocks off the ASX Screener, there are Shariah-compliant ETFs listed on the ASX — but as of today, every single one of them comes from one issuer: Hejaz Financial Services.

TickerFundWhat it holds
ISLMHejaz Equities Fund Active ETFGlobal equities, screened to AAOIFI standards. Benchmarked to the MSCI World Islamic Index (hedged to AUD) — this is a global fund, not an ASX-only one; recent disclosed holdings include names like Taiwan Semiconductor and AMD alongside BlueScope Steel.
HJZPHejaz Property Fund Active ETFGlobal property assets.
SKUKHejaz Sukuk Active ETFSukuk (Islamic bonds) — asset-backed certificates structured to avoid interest, as an alternative to conventional fixed income.
HJHIHejaz High Income Active ETFListed high-dividend companies, screened.
HHIFHejaz High Innovation Active ETFListed companies in innovation-focused sectors, screened.

Who certifies them

Hejaz's funds are independently certified by Minarah Consulting, an external Sharia Supervisory Board, and Hejaz is an institutional member of AAOIFI. Hejaz publishes an actual Shariah compliance certificate for its super and investment products — worth reading directly rather than taking a marketing page's word for it: Hejaz Shariah Certificate (PDF).

The single-issuer problem

Every ASX-listed halal ETF currently available is a Hejaz product. That's not a knock on Hejaz specifically — it's a genuine structural risk worth naming: no competing issuer, no alternative if you want diversification of certification methodology or management, and all five funds share the same house view on what counts as compliant. If a global provider like BlackRock or Vanguard ever lists a Shariah-screened ETF on the ASX, that changes; as of this guide, it hasn't happened. The same single-issuer point applies even more starkly to sukuk — see our sukuk in Australia guide.

A claim we checked and rejected: several search results (repeated near-identically across sources) describe a "SPDR S&P/ASX 200 Islamic ETF" tracking the S&P/ASX 200 Shariah Index. We could confirm the index is real — S&P Dow Jones Indices launched S&P/ASX Shariah indices in March 2023 — but we could not find a verified, ASX-listed ticker for a product that actually tracks it, and the claim conflicts with Hejaz's own disclosed fund data for ISLM (which tracks a different, global index, not the ASX 200 Shariah index). We're not publishing a ticker we can't stand behind. If this changes, this guide will be updated.

Whichever route you take — individual stocks or an ETF wrapper — the same diligence applies: check the current PDS and Target Market Determination, confirm who's actually doing the Shariah screening and certification, and don't assume "Islamic" or "halal" in a fund's name is self-certifying.
