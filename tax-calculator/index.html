Work out your real take-home pay

Income tax, Medicare levy, HECS-HELP and super — broken down bracket by bracket, not just a final number. Knowing what's actually left over is the first step to working out what you can invest, save, or owe in zakat.

New to this? How Australian income tax actually works

The biggest misconception: your whole income is not taxed at your top rate. Australia uses marginal brackets — you pay 0% on the first $18,200, then 15% on the next slice, then 30% on the next, and so on. Only the portion of your income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. Someone earning $90,000 doesn't pay 30% on all $90,000 — most of it sits in lower brackets taxed at 0% and 15%.

Medicare levy is a separate 2% on top of income tax, funding Medicare. HECS-HELP is only relevant if you have a university/vocational study loan — it's not extra tax, it's compulsory repayment of your own debt once you earn above a threshold. Super is money your employer puts into your retirement account — by law, at least 12% of your salary, on top of what you're paid (unless your contract specifically says your package "includes" super, which is worth checking).

This calculator assumes you're an Australian resident for tax purposes, with no dependents — residency and family situation change the numbers, so if either applies to you, treat this as a rough guide and check the ATO or a tax agent for your exact figures.

Advanced: dependants, family situation & salary sacrifice

Having a spouse or dependent children raises your Medicare levy and surcharge thresholds — meaning you can earn more before either kicks in. This applies even without a partner: a sole parent with dependent children still gets the family threshold.

Reduces your taxable income and adds directly to your super instead. Total concessional (pre-tax) super contributions — including the standard 12% — are capped at roughly $30,000/year; going over is taxed differently, so check the current cap on the ATO site before relying on a large figure here.

Note on accuracy: if you have a spouse, we apply the family Medicare/surcharge thresholds to your own income only — we don't ask for your partner's income, so this won't match a fully combined household assessment. For that, use the ATO's own calculator.
