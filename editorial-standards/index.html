Editorial Standards

How this site is made

Where the numbers come from, what gets checked by hand, where AI was used and where it wasn't, and what happens when something here turns out to be wrong.

Why this page exists. Most finance sites don't tell you how their content was produced. Google's own guidance for site owners asks three questions — who made this, how was it made, and why. This page answers the second one honestly, including the parts that are less flattering.

Where the screening figures come from

Every ratio on this site — debt, cash and interest-bearing securities, and accounts receivable, each measured against market capitalisation — is calculated from figures in a company's own published financial statements. Market capitalisation is taken at the time of the screening pass. Nothing is licensed from a third-party screening vendor, and there is no proprietary score: the methodology page publishes every threshold and links the primary AAOIFI, S&P and MSCI source documents so you can reproduce any verdict yourself.

Figures are re-checked quarterly. Every screened stock page shows the ratios it was assessed on, because a balance sheet moves and a verdict is a point-in-time reading rather than a permanent label.

Where AI was used, and where it wasn't

This site is built and maintained by one person, and AI tooling was used substantially in producing it. Being specific about that rather than vague:

AI was used for

Drafting and editing prose, building the site itself — the screener, calculators and page generation — gathering candidate sources, and doing first-pass research that was then checked.

AI was not the final word on

Which AAOIFI thresholds apply, whether a source is credible enough to cite, whether a disputed fiqh question gets a definitive answer (it doesn't), or whether a claim that couldn't be verified gets published anyway (it doesn't).

What that means for you

Treat the arithmetic as reproducible and the sources as checkable — both are published precisely so you don't have to take anyone's word for it. Treat any religious ruling as a starting point for a conversation with a scholar, not a substitute for one.

Google's spam policies target content produced at scale to manipulate rankings rather than to help people, regardless of whether a human or a machine typed it. That's the right standard, and it's the one this site tries to hold itself to. The test isn't whether AI touched a page — it's whether the page tells you something true and useful that you couldn't easily get elsewhere.

Rules this site follows

Unverifiable claims don't get published

A widely-repeated claim about an ASX-listed Islamic ETF tracking the S&P/ASX 200 Shariah Index appears across multiple sources. It could not be substantiated against any issuer's own disclosures, and it conflicted with the fund data that is published. It was left out, and the reasoning was published instead of the claim. This happens more often than you'd expect in Islamic finance content, where claims get copied between sites without anyone checking the source.

Disputed questions are presented as disputed

Where credentialed scholars genuinely disagree — whether Australian Islamic home finance is compliant in substance, whether nisab should follow the gold or silver standard, how zakat applies to modern assets — both positions are set out and neither is quietly adopted as house view. A site that resolves live scholarly disagreements on your behalf is overstepping.

Negative findings get reported

Where the Australian market doesn't offer a good option, that's stated. Retail sukuk access is genuinely thin. Takaful barely exists here in practice. Reporting a gap is more useful than padding a page with alternatives that don't really work.

Corrections are made in public

When something here is wrong it gets fixed and the correction is stated, not silently edited away. If you find an error, the fastest way to get it fixed is to send the filing reference — the methodology page shows where each line is drawn, which usually identifies exactly where a disagreement sits.

Nothing here is sold

No accounts, no paywalled content, no sponsored placements, no paid rankings. Providers appear because they exist and are relevant. If that ever changes — an affiliate arrangement, for example — it gets disclosed on the page where it applies.

What this site is not

It is not financial advice and it is not a fatwa. Screening a stock against a published standard is a factual exercise; deciding what you should do with your money, or what is permissible in your circumstances, is not. For anything consequential the right move is a licensed adviser and a qualified scholar.
